# ICP Examples — Quality Bar (Context)

Two examples showing the depth and structure the ICPBuilder node must match. Every field grounded in the evidence pack or clearly reasoned from the business model — never generic filler. 2–3 segments per report; each segment ~150–250 words.

## Example 1 — B2B ICP
*(For a fictional cybersecurity compliance consultancy selling SOC 2 / ISO 27001 readiness to software companies.)*

**Segment: The funding-deadline founder**
- **Firmographics:** B2B SaaS companies, 15–60 employees, seed to Series A, selling to mid-market or enterprise customers. Usually US/UK/EU, remote-first.
- **Decision maker:** Founder/CEO or CTO. No dedicated compliance or security hire yet — that's precisely why they need outside help.
- **Trigger events:** A prospect's procurement team requested a SOC 2 report · an enterprise deal stalled on a security questionnaire · an investor flagged compliance in due diligence. The trigger is always a deal or a raise, never abstract "security maturity".
- **Pain points:** Deals slipping quarter to quarter; engineers pulled off product to answer questionnaires; no idea whether this takes three months or twelve.
- **Objections:** "Can't we just use an automated platform like Vanta and skip consultants?" · "How much engineering time will this really take?" · fear of an open-ended engagement.
- **Buying behavior:** Researches under deadline pressure; shortlists fast; picks the vendor who gives the clearest timeline and fixed boundary. Trust signals that matter: named auditor relationships, client logos in their industry, a realistic timeline stated up front.
- **Best channels:** Founder-to-founder LinkedIn · search ("SOC 2 timeline startup") · referrals from VCs and auditors · communities where technical founders compare notes.

## Example 2 — B2C ICP
*(For a fictional D2C ergonomic furniture brand selling standing desks and chairs, mid-premium price point, India + GCC markets.)*

**Segment: The WFH professional with a paining back**
- **Demographics:** 28–45, tech/finance/creative professionals, household income comfortably mid-to-upper band, metro cities, working hybrid or fully remote 3+ years.
- **Psychographics:** Treats their home office as career infrastructure, not furniture. Researches heavily; reads reviews and comparison videos before spending. Values durability and health claims backed by specifics, distrusts influencer hype.
- **Trigger events:** New back/neck pain or a physiotherapist visit · moving house · a raise or bonus that unlocks the purchase they'd been postponing · seeing a colleague's setup on a video call.
- **Pain points:** Cheap chair bought in 2021 is causing real discomfort; overwhelmed by identical-looking options; worried about buying premium without trying in person.
- **Objections:** "Is this genuinely better than one costing a third as much?" · delivery and assembly hassle · return policy anxiety for a bulky item.
- **Buying behavior:** 2–6 week consideration window, multiple site visits before purchase, abandons cart to compare, converts on remarketing or a concrete guarantee (trial period, free returns).
- **Best channels:** Search (high-intent comparison queries) · YouTube reviews · Instagram/Meta remarketing (not cold prospecting) · WhatsApp for order support and cart recovery in these markets.

## Rules the ICPBuilder must follow
1. Trigger events are the most valuable field — they define timing and message. Never omit them; never make them vague ("wants to grow").
2. Objections must be ones this business would actually hear, phrased as the buyer would say them.
3. Channel recommendations must connect to the segment's behavior, not a generic channel list.
4. If the business is "both" B2B and B2C, lead with the higher-revenue-potential side and cover the other in one segment.
5. Where the evidence pack is thin, say so: mark low-confidence fields as "to validate in discovery" rather than inventing specifics.
