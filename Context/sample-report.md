## Growth Snapshot — Brightpath HR Solutions

**What we understood.** You're an eight-person HR consultancy working with UK SMEs, mostly on retained HR support and one-off projects like handbooks and restructures. Your work comes almost entirely from referrals, and you want a second, predictable source of leads within the next quarter.

**What we found.**

- Your site lists services but never names outcomes. The retained-support page describes what you do ("ongoing HR advice, documentation, compliance updates") but not what a client avoids or gains. Buyers comparing you against Peninsula or a freelancer have nothing to weigh. *(Source: /services page copy.)*
- There is no analytics or conversion tracking installed. We found no GA4, no tag manager, no form tracking. Right now you cannot know which pages produce enquiries, which makes every marketing decision a guess. *(Source: site scan.)*
- Your only call to action is a generic contact form, and it asks seven questions. Forms this long typically suppress enquiries from busy directors. There is no low-commitment option — no guide, no checklist, no booking link. *(Source: /contact.)*
- You publish nothing. The blog's last post is from 2024. For searches like "HR outsourcing for small business Manchester", your competitors' pages appear; yours don't. *(Source: site scan + your answer that content stopped "when things got busy".)*
- Your LinkedIn page posts sporadically, but your founder profile has real engagement history when it was active. In your market, founder-led LinkedIn typically outperforms company pages for generating conversations. *(Source: public LinkedIn activity.)*

**Who you should be targeting.** Based on your client base and market, two segments stand out:

*Segment 1 — the outgrown founder.* Owner or MD of a 25–80 person firm (professional services, light manufacturing, agencies) that has grown past DIY HR. Trigger events: a first tribunal scare, a bad hire, hitting the headcount where policies become unavoidable. Objection: "we're not big enough for real HR support." Best channels: founder-to-founder LinkedIn, referral partners (accountants, solicitors), search.

*Segment 2 — the stretched ops lead.* Operations or finance manager in an 80–200 person company where HR is one of their five jobs. Trigger: compliance change, restructure, or simply drowning. Objection: cost versus hiring in-house. Best channels: search (they research before asking), email once identified, LinkedIn.

**Possible routes.**

*Route A — fix the leaks first, then drive traffic.* Install tracking, rewrite the three main service pages around outcomes, cut the form to three fields, add a booking link and one downloadable guide ("The 30-minute HR health check"). Effort: low to medium. Realistic milestones: tracking live and form simplified within 2–3 weeks; rewritten pages and the guide live within 4–6 weeks, assuming your input on client outcomes arrives promptly. Trade-off: this route generates no new traffic by itself — it makes sure the traffic you already have, and everything Routes B or C would send, actually converts. If you do nothing here, any spend on visibility leaks. Much of this route you could do in-house if someone owns it.

*Route B — founder-led outbound.* Build a validated list of Segment 1 companies in the North West, and run a founder-to-founder outreach sequence: connection, one useful message, one soft offer of the guide. Effort: medium. Realistic milestones: list built and sequence written within 2–3 weeks; but note that email infrastructure needs 4–6 weeks of setup and warm-up before meaningful volume, and reply rates typically need a further 2–4 weeks of calibration. So treat this as a quarter-long build, not a month-one result. Trade-off: fastest path to actual conversations; depends on your founder's willingness to be visible and reply personally.

*Route C — own the searches.* A content and local SEO push targeting the questions both segments search: HR outsourcing costs, tribunal risk, policy requirements at 50 staff. Effort: medium to high, sustained. Honest timeline: organic search takes 3–6+ months to move; the first 4–6 weeks are foundation (technical fixes, page optimization, first articles), not rankings. Trade-off: slowest to start, but it compounds — and it's the only route your competitors can't easily copy overnight. Skip it for now if the next 90 days matter more than the next 2 years; sequence it after A if both matter.

**Quick wins (inside 30 days, any route).**
1. Install GA4 and form tracking — you can't improve what you can't see.
2. Cut the contact form to name, email, and one question.
3. Add a meeting-booking link to the site and your founder's LinkedIn profile.
4. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile — it's currently unclaimed.
5. Repost your two best-performing old LinkedIn posts with updated numbers; founder profile, not company page.

**Assumptions.** We assumed your capacity to take on roughly 3–5 new retained clients per quarter, that your founder can invest 2–3 hours weekly in LinkedIn if Route B runs, and that page copy inputs come from your team within a week of request. If any of these are off, the sequencing above changes.
