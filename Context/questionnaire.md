# Catalyst Client Discovery Questionnaire (Context)

Transcribed from the internal Client Interview Questionnaire. Part 2 (the 45–60 min live interview guide) is intentionally omitted — it belongs to the human discovery call, not the SaaS. Part 1 seeds the intake wizard; Part 3 seeds ModuleConfig; Part 4 defines the InternalArtifact schema.

## Part 1 · Pre-Discovery Form (seeds Stages 0–1 of the intake)

**Section A — About your business**
1. What is your company name and website? (short text; the URL drives Stage 0 pre-fill)
2. What industry are you in, and what do you sell or provide? (short text; confirm-or-correct from scrape)
3. How large is your team, and where do you operate? (dropdown 1–10 / 11–50 / 51–200 / 200+ plus locations)
4. Who will attend the discovery session, and what are their roles? (call-stage only — not in SaaS intake)
5. May we record and transcribe the discovery call for analysis? (call-stage only; consent control)

**Section B — Your goal**
6. In your own words, what is the main problem or opportunity you want help with? (long text, required — the unprompted framing anchors everything)
7. If this engagement succeeds, what will be different in 6 to 12 months? (long text; surfaces expected outcome and success-metric candidates)
8. What have you already tried, internally or with other providers? (optional; reveals constraints and sensitivities)

**Section C — Services of interest**
9. Which areas are you interested in? Select all that apply. (checkboxes, required — options are the 13 families plus "Not sure, help me decide"; routes the conditional modules. "Not sure" is valid; diagnosis decides the mapping.)

**Section D — Context**
10. Which tools and platforms do you currently use? (website platform, CRM, analytics, advertising, email, AI tools — optional)
11. When do you want work to start, and is there a fixed deadline or event driving the timing? (ASAP / within 1 month / 1–3 months / exploring + short text, required)
12. Do you have a budget range in mind for this initiative? (broad ranges + "prefer to discuss", optional — budget context only, never a quote)
13. How are decisions like this approved in your company? (short text, optional; follow-up: purchase order or procurement process involved?)
14. Is there anything you want us to review before the call? (links, documents, reports — file upload, optional; builds the evidence pack)

## Part 3 · Conditional Service Modules (seeds ModuleConfig)
Ask a module only if the client selected that service in Q9 (or discovery clearly points to it). All module questions optional unless marked required. 3–5 questions each.

**Module STR · AI strategy & digital growth consulting**
- STR-1. "Where do you suspect AI could save cost or time in your business, even as a guess?" (open; follow-up: what has stopped you from trying it? — locates AI-readiness starting points and adoption blockers)
- STR-2. "Do you have a written growth or marketing plan today, or does the team act tactically?" (classify, required — distinguishes strategy engagement from execution one)
- STR-3. "Which competitors do you watch, and what do they do better online?" (capture names — seeds competitor research and positioning)
- STR-4. "If we audited one thing first, should it be your funnel, your tech stack, or your AI readiness?" (pick one + why — prioritizes the first audit workstream)

**Module WEB · Website design & development**
- WEB-1. "Is this a new site, a redesign, or fixes to the current site?" (pick one, required; follow-up: what platform is the current site on, and must we keep it? — separates bounded build work from ongoing improvement, constrains platform choice)
- WEB-2. "What is the site's main job: leads, sales, bookings, credibility, self-service?" (pick primary, required — the conversion goal drives structure, copy, measurement)
- WEB-3. "Roughly how many pages or page types, and who provides copy and images?" (capture numbers; follow-up: do integrations exist (CRM, booking, payments)? — sizes the build, flags content and integration dependencies)
- WEB-4. "Who will update the site after launch, and how technical are they?" (open — decides CMS choice, training needs, whether a care plan belongs in the recommendation)

**Module SEO · SEO & AI search visibility**
- SEO-1. "What should someone type into Google, or ask ChatGPT, to find you? Do you show up today?" (capture phrases, required — defines target queries and the visibility gap across classic and AI search)
- SEO-2. "Is your market local, national, or international?" (pick one, required; follow-up: which cities or regions matter most? — routes local/national/ecommerce SEO approaches)
- SEO-3. "Have you done SEO work before, in-house or with an agency? What happened?" (open; follow-up: any history of penalties, migrations, lost rankings? — history reveals technical debt and risk before an audit)
- SEO-4. "Do you publish content today: blog, guides, videos? Who writes it?" (open — content capacity decides whether content production belongs in scope)

**Module ADS · Paid advertising & performance marketing**
- ADS-1. "Are you running ads now? On which platforms, at roughly what monthly spend?" (capture per platform, required — current spend and mix frame the audit and realistic starting point)
- ADS-2. "What does a lead or sale from ads cost you today, if you know? What would make ads clearly worth it?" (capture CPL/CPA if known; follow-up: how is a lead's value calculated? — establishes the economics that define success before any campaign is proposed)
- ADS-3. "Where do ad clicks land today, and can we change those pages?" (open, required — landing-page control decides whether CRO or page builds must join the scope)
- ADS-4. "Is conversion tracking set up, and do you trust it?" (yes/no/unsure, required — broken tracking must be fixed first; classic Phase 1 quick win)

**Module SOC · Social media marketing & content creation**
- SOC-1. "Which platforms are you active on, and which actually bring customers?" (open per platform, required — separates presence from performance, guides platform selection)
- SOC-2. "Who creates content today, and how often do you post?" (open; follow-up: can anyone on your team appear on camera? — production capacity and on-camera availability shape content plan and video formats)
- SOC-3. "Who replies to comments and DMs today, and how fast?" (open — reveals whether community management belongs in scope)
- SOC-4. "Have you worked with creators or used customer content before?" (open — gauges appetite for influencer and UGC coordination)

**Module CON · Content marketing & copywriting**
- CON-1. "Which content do you need most: website copy, blog articles, emails, sales pages, case studies?" (checkboxes, required — identifies formats and volumes for scoping)
- CON-2. "Do you have a documented brand voice or examples of copy you love and hate?" (open, collect examples — voice references cut revision rounds dramatically)
- CON-3. "Who reviews and approves content, and how long does a review take today?" (open, required — approval cycle time drives realistic publishing cadence)
- CON-4. "How do you feel about AI-assisted drafting with human editing?" (open — sets expectations for production method and any client policies on AI content)

**Module BRD · Branding & creative services**
- BRD-1. "Is this a new brand, a refresh, or new assets within the existing brand?" (pick one, required — scopes strategy-level work versus production work)
- BRD-2. "What should your brand say about you that it does not today?" (open; follow-up: which brands, in any industry, feel right to you? — captures positioning intent and taste references for the creative brief)
- BRD-3. "What exists already: logo files, guidelines, templates, messaging?" (checklist — inventory decides what is built versus reused)
- BRD-4. "Which deliverables matter first: identity, guidelines, pitch deck, brochures, ad creatives, social kit?" (rank top three, required — ranks bounded deliverables for a phased, task-based scope)

**Module AIA · AI automation & AI solutions**
- AIA-1. "Which repetitive tasks eat the most team time each week?" (open, list with rough hours, required — ranks automation candidates by recoverable hours for the impact matrix)
- AIA-2. "How do customer inquiries arrive (web, WhatsApp, phone, email), and what happens if one arrives at midnight?" (open, required — reveals speed-to-lead gaps that chatbots or auto-responses could close)
- AIA-3. "Which systems would an automation need to touch: CRM, email, calendar, documents, databases?" (checklist; follow-up: do those tools have admin access available to us? — integration surface determines complexity, risk, and required specialists)
- AIA-4. "Are there answers a bot must never give, or data it must never see?" (open, required — safety and data boundaries are design inputs, not afterthoughts)

**Module APP · Mobile app & software development**
- APP-1. "Describe the product in one sentence: who uses it, to do what?" (open, required; follow-up: is this a new product or a replacement for spreadsheets and manual work? — the one-sentence test exposes scope clarity instantly)
- APP-2. "What is the smallest version that would be genuinely useful on day one?" (open, list must-have features, required — defines the MVP boundary; everything else is roadmap, not scope)
- APP-3. "Does it need to connect to existing systems: payments, CRM, WhatsApp, internal databases?" (checklist — integrations dominate effort and risk in software estimates)
- APP-4. "Who maintains it after launch, and what uptime or support expectations do you have?" (open, required — separates the build from run-and-maintain; points to hybrid commercial structures)

**Module ECOM · Ecommerce & marketplace growth**
- ECOM-1. "Do you sell online today? On what platform, with roughly how many products and orders per month?" (capture numbers, required — store maturity and volume set the baseline and the right first workstream)
- ECOM-2. "Where do you think you lose buyers: traffic, product pages, cart, checkout, repeat purchases?" (pick suspected leak; follow-up: do you follow up on abandoned carts today? — the suspected leak, checked against data, prioritizes CRO versus acquisition versus retention)
- ECOM-3. "What are your margins and average order value, roughly?" (ranges fine, optional — unit economics decide which growth levers can be profitable)
- ECOM-4. "Do marketplaces matter to you: Amazon or others, now or next?" (open — flags marketplace strategy as a separate workstream)

**Module DATA · Data, analytics & reporting**
- DATA-1. "What decisions do you struggle to make because the data is missing or untrusted?" (open, required — anchors measurement design in decisions, not dashboards for their own sake)
- DATA-2. "Where does your data live today: analytics, CRM, ad platforms, spreadsheets, finance tools?" (checklist, required — source inventory scopes the tracking and dashboard work)
- DATA-3. "Who needs to see reports, and how often?" (open, per audience — audiences and cadence define the reporting deliverable)
- DATA-4. "Can you tell today which marketing channel produces revenue, not just clicks?" (yes/no/partly, required — attribution capability is the usual gap; fixing it is often the first quick win)

**Module LOC · Online reputation & local business growth**
- LOC-1. "How many locations or service areas do you have, and which matter most?" (open, list, required — location count scales the whole local program)
- LOC-2. "Is your Google Business Profile claimed and managed? By whom?" (yes/no/unsure, required — profile ownership is the access dependency everything local depends on)
- LOC-3. "How do you get reviews today, and how do you handle negative ones?" (open; follow-up: what is your current rating versus your main local rival? — review volume, response practice, and the competitive gap scope the reputation work)
- LOC-4. "When a local customer calls or fills a form, what happens next, and how fast?" (open — local leads convert on speed; reveals follow-up and automation gaps)

**Module RET · Ongoing maintenance & retainers**
- RET-1. "Which assets need ongoing care: website, SEO, ads, content, social, automations, analytics?" (checklist, required — defines the retainer surface and the specialist mix)
- RET-2. "Who handles this today, and what breaks or slips most often?" (open, required — current failure modes define the retainer's first responsibilities)
- RET-3. "How quickly do you expect a response when something breaks, versus a routine request?" (open, capture expectations; follow-up: is out-of-hours coverage ever needed? — response expectations shape the capacity plan and are agreed in the SOW, never assumed)
- RET-4. "Beyond keeping things running, what should improve month over month?" (open, required — separates maintenance from growth retainers and sets the monthly reporting focus)

## Part 4 · Post-Interview Summary (the InternalArtifact schema)
Completed within 48 hours of interview (or generation, for tool-originated leads). AI-assisted analysis, human validation sign-off mandatory before anything is delivered. Sections:

1. **Opportunity qualification summary** — fit with service families (strong / partial / weak), problem clarity (clear / emerging / vague), decision maker engaged (yes / no), budget context (approved / case needed / unknown), urgency (hard date / soft / exploring), recommendation: proceed to Blueprint / run paid scoping phase / refer / decline with reason.
2. **Recommended Catalyst service workstreams** — each with evidence behind it (quote or data point from the evidence pack), mapped to the 13 families, tagged quick win / core build / optimize & scale. No workstream without evidence.
3. **Missing information** — open questions, unverified numbers, access not yet confirmed, stakeholders not yet met. Assign each item an owner and earliest date; anything blocking must be flagged. (For the SaaS: this list becomes the suggested discovery-call agenda.)
4. **Recommended commercial model** — apply the decision tree: scope stable and bounded with clear acceptance → task-based; ongoing capacity, changing priorities, recurring optimization → managed capacity; continuous operations plus defined projects → hybrid; still uncertain → paid or separately approved scoping phase. Record answers to Q1–Q3 and the logic.
5. **Required roles and skills** — roles, seniority, indicative allocation per workstream (growth strategist, developer per platform, SEO specialist, ads expert, automation specialist, copywriter, analyst, QA, delivery manager).
6. **Key assumptions, dependencies, and risks** — client-side dependencies (access, content, approvals), third parties, delivery risks; each with a proposed control. RAID-seeded.
7. **Inputs for the Catalyst Solution Blueprint** — executive summary draft (three sentences, client's language), business needs and root causes, quick wins, phased roadmap skeleton, measurement plan candidates.

Reminder embedded in the template: human validation is mandatory before anything reaches the client; all prices are custom quotes scoped after discovery; transcripts and client data are handled confidentially.
